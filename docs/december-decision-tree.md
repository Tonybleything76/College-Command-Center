# The December Decision

**Read this in December. Not before.**

Everything here depends on one thing you won't know until mid-December: what Notre
Dame says. This exists so that when the answer comes, you already know what happens
next — instead of trying to figure it out over winter break in a panic.

---

## How the timing works

| When | What |
|---|---|
| **Nov 1, 2026** | Notre Dame REA application submitted |
| **Early–mid Dec 2026** | Holy Cross interview (request it back in October) |
| **Dec 20, 2026** | Holy Cross net cost known — see "Before any of this" below |
| **Mid-Dec 2026** | **Notre Dame responds** |
| **Jan 15, 2027** | Holy Cross Early Decision II deadline — **binding** |
| **Feb 15, 2027** | Holy Cross ED II decision |
| **Late March 2027** | Notre Dame Regular Decision, if you were deferred |
| **May 1, 2027** | Deposit deadline everywhere |

**What "binding" means:** if you apply Early Decision II to Holy Cross and they admit
you, you have committed to attend. You withdraw every other application, including
Notre Dame. That's the whole trade — better odds in exchange for giving up the choice.

---

## Before any of this happens

Three things have to be done by **December 20**, or the tree doesn't work:

1. **Holy Cross net price calculator, run with real numbers.** ED II binds you in
   January, but the actual aid package doesn't arrive until February. You'd be
   committing without knowing the cost. The calculator is the only way to have a real
   number before you sign. **Don't skip this one.**
2. **Holy Cross interview done.** They say interviews are "highly recommended" and
   they use them in the evaluation. Request it in October; slots fill.
3. **Holy Cross supplements drafted.** You need them for ED II *or* Regular Decision,
   so the writing happens in December either way. December is the easiest writing
   window of your whole year — no musical, four applications already done.

---

## The three branches

### 1. Notre Dame says YES

**Holy Cross ED II is off the table.** ED II is binding, and you can't bind yourself to
Holy Cross while holding a Notre Dame acceptance.

**What to do:** apply to Holy Cross **Regular Decision** anyway, if you want to compare
money in the spring. Both schools may meet full need, and two real aid offers side by
side in April is worth one application in December.

**Don't deposit anywhere until every aid offer is in.** You have until May 1. Use it.

---

### 2. Notre Dame says NO

**Holy Cross ED II is your strongest remaining move** — better odds at a school you
actually want.

One condition: **the net cost has to work.** You ran the calculator on December 20.

- **Number looks affordable** → apply ED II by January 15.
- **Number doesn't work** → apply Regular Decision instead. Keep your options open and
  compare real offers in the spring. Binding yourself to a school you can't afford is
  the one genuinely bad outcome available here.

---

### 3. Notre Dame says MAYBE — deferred

This is the hard one, and it's the reason this document exists.

**Deferred means you're still alive at Notre Dame.** Your application rolls into
Regular Decision and you'd hear again in late March.

**But ED II at Holy Cross is binding.** If you apply January 15 and they admit you
February 15, you withdraw from Notre Dame — before Notre Dame ever gives you its real
answer.

So the question is genuinely uncomfortable: **do you trade a likely Holy Cross for a
maybe at Notre Dame?**

**Things that should push you toward taking the Holy Cross ED II:**
- The net cost works and you know it
- You can picture yourself happy there — not as a consolation, as a real place
- Deferred applicants at highly selective schools are admitted at low rates in the
  regular round *(verify Notre Dame's actual deferral numbers before relying on this)*

**Things that should push you toward waiting:**
- Notre Dame is unambiguously your first choice and you'd always wonder
- Holy Cross's cost is still unknown or uncomfortable
- You'd rather have the real answer than the safer one

**There is no wrong answer here.** One is a good outcome you choose; the other is a
better outcome you might not get. That's a values question, not a strategy question,
and it's yours.

**If you wait:** apply Holy Cross **Regular Decision**, and send Notre Dame a **letter
of continued interest** — a short, specific note saying you'd still enroll and what's
happened since you applied. *(Verify Notre Dame accepts these and how they want them
sent.)*

---

## The one rule that covers all three branches

**Do not commit to anything binding until you know what it costs.**

Everything else in here is a judgment call. This one isn't.
