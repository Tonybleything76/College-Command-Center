What this is

Nine college applications, broken into small pieces, in the order they actually need to happen. It keeps track so you don't have to hold it all in your head.

The whole idea: you open this, it tells you the two or three things that matter today, you do one of them, you close it. That's it. It will never show you all 21 things at once unless you go looking for them.

One rule about the essays

Nothing here writes your essays for you. It asks you questions, shows you your own answers, and tells you which one is the essay — then you write it.

Two reasons. Several of these schools make you sign that the writing is yours. And essays written by somebody else read like everyone else's, which is the fastest way to be forgotten. Yours won't be.

What things actually cost

Sticker price is fiction. The expensive schools can end up cheapest — the ones that cost $90K meet 100% of need and the ones that cost $30K don't. Don't cross anything off because the top number scared you.

The question that decides everything

Does Notre Dame's free tuition actually apply?

They cover full tuition under $150,000 — but they require the Noncustodial Parent CSS Profile, so both parents' incomes count together, not just the one you live with.

Nobody can answer this until both profiles are filed. It's the difference between Notre Dame being the cheapest school here and one of the most expensive. Parents' job, not yours.

Music money — read once

Nearly every music scholarship requires declaring the music major. Holy Cross Brooks is full tuition, majors only. Madison's waiver, majors only. NYU Steinhardt, majors only.

Still open without majoring: singing in the ensembles — almost everywhere, including Madison's jazz groups and the Holy Cross jazz combo. And Eau Claire, where jazz is open to every student.

What that means: you don't need to chase auditions at eight schools this fall. That whole workstream is off your plate.

Who to call

Emails you can steal

Asking a music department a question

Subject: Prescreen timing — prospective first-year, jazz voice

Hi — I'm a senior applying for fall 2027 and I'm interested in jazz voice. Two questions: when is the prescreen deadline for next fall's auditions, and are students who aren't music majors eligible to audition for scholarships or ensembles? Thank you — [name], [school], [city]

Short. Two specific questions. They answer short emails.

Requesting an interview

Subject: Interview request — first-year applicant

Hi — I'm applying as a first-year student for fall 2027 and I'd like to request an interview. I'm available [two or three real windows]. Thank you — [name], [school], [city]

Send in October. Slots fill.

What not to send

Don't email to say you submitted. Don't email to say you're still interested with nothing else in the message. Don't email weekly. Contact them when you have a real question or real news. Anything else reads as anxious, and admissions offices notice.

The December decision

Notre Dame says yes → Holy Cross ED II is off. Apply Regular there if you want two aid offers to compare.

Notre Dame says no → Holy Cross ED II is your strongest move, if the cost works.

Deferred → the hard one. You're still alive at Notre Dame, but ED II is binding: get into Holy Cross and you withdraw from Notre Dame before they ever give you a real answer. That's a values question, not a strategy question, and it's yours.

The one rule: don't commit to anything binding until you know what it costs.
