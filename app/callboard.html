Essay workbench

These are yours to write. What's here is questions to get you unstuck and the real prompts so you're not guessing. Nobody writes them for you — several of these schools make you sign that you wrote your own. And ghostwritten essays read like everyone else's. Yours don't.

Start here · 75 words

"How does faith influence the decisions you make?" Notre Dame · 50–100 words · pick 2 of 4 · this is one

Don't write an essay. Answer the question. One decision, what you did, why faith was in the room.

Questions to get you started
  • Name one decision this year where faith actually changed what you did — not one you'd have made anyway.
  • What do you believe that costs you something?
  • When you prepare to preach, what are you trying to do to the people listening?
  • Where have you doubted, and what did you do with it? Doubt isn't disqualifying at Notre Dame. It's interesting.

Failure mode: describing faith in general. They know what faith is. They want to know what yours does.

Notre Dame · required · 150 words

"Everyone has different priorities when considering their higher education options and building their college or university list. Tell us about your 'non-negotiable' factor(s) when searching for your future college home." Notre Dame · 150 words max · required
What they're really asking

Whether you know yourself well enough to have real criteria — and whether those criteria happen to describe them.

Failure mode: listing what's great about Notre Dame. They know. This is about you, and the fit should be obvious without you pointing at it.

Angles that fit you: somewhere faith gets discussed rather than assumed; somewhere you can do science seriously and sing seriously without picking; a place small enough that showing up changes something.

150 words is short. One non-negotiable done well beats three listed.

#4 is the sleeper. Almost nobody answers it well, and it's where someone with a preaching background can land a punch in eighty words. It rewards conviction.

Whichever you take, it should do something the faith answer doesn't. Two answers about service wastes one of them.

Common App personal statement · 650 words

Good enough to build on by Sept 30. Not finished. Not perfect. Good enough that supplements can start. This one date governs everything else.

It goes to all nine schools, so it's the most valuable thing you'll write. It's also the hardest — which is exactly why it isn't first on your call.

You have four rough drafts. Start there.

Before writing anything new, mark each one: alive (something real in here, even if the writing is bad), dead (the topic isn't going anywhere), or wrong shape (good material, wrong story around it).

One alive draft saves two weeks. Be honest, not loyal.

Starting fresh — questions, not prompts
  • What were you wrong about, and what changed your mind?
  • When have you been the only one who noticed something?
  • What do you do when nobody assigns it?
  • A moment on stage, or preaching, where something real happened that wasn't in the script.
  • Where do the pieces of you not fit together? Science and jazz. Softball and preaching. That tension is usually the essay.

What makes these work: a small specific moment you can actually see, then honest thinking about it. Not an achievement list. Not a lesson learned.

The question that decides everything

Does Notre Dame's free tuition actually apply?

They cover full tuition under $150,000 — but they require the Noncustodial Parent CSS Profile, which means both parents' incomes count together, not just the one you live with.

Nobody can answer this until both profiles are filed. It's the difference between Notre Dame being the cheapest school here and one of the most expensive. Parents' job, not yours.

Emails you can steal

Asking a music department a question

Subject: Prescreen timing — prospective first-year, jazz voice

Hi — I'm a senior applying for fall 2027 and I'm interested in jazz voice. Two questions: when is the prescreen deadline for next fall's auditions, and are students who aren't music majors eligible to audition for scholarships or ensembles? Thank you — [name], [school], [city]

Short. Two specific questions. They answer short emails.

Requesting an interview

Subject: Interview request — first-year applicant

Hi — I'm applying as a first-year student for fall 2027 and I'd like to request an interview. I'm available [two or three real windows]. Thank you — [name], [school], [city]

Send in October. Slots fill.

What not to send

Don't email to say you submitted. Don't email to say you're still interested with nothing else in the message. Don't email weekly. Contact them when you have a real question or real news. Anything else reads as anxious, and admissions offices notice.

The December decision

Notre Dame says yes → Holy Cross ED II is off. Apply Regular there if you want two aid offers to compare.

Notre Dame says no → Holy Cross ED II is your strongest move, if the cost works.

Deferred → the hard one. You're still alive at Notre Dame, but ED II is binding: get into Holy Cross and you withdraw from Notre Dame before they ever give you a real answer. That's a values question, not a strategy question, and it's yours.

The one rule: don't commit to anything binding until you know what it costs.
