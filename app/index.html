Essay workbench

These are yours to write. What's here is questions to get you unstuck, structure to push against, and the real prompts so you're not guessing. Nobody writes them for you — several of these schools make you sign that you wrote your own. Also, essays written by someone else read like everyone else's. Yours don't.

Start here: 75 words

Notre Dame short answer — the faith one

"How does faith influence the decisions you make?"
50–100 words. Notre Dame. Pick 2 of 4 — this is one of them.

You're on the preaching team. You've said things like this out loud, in front of people, and they landed. This is the easiest real thing on your entire list — and it's for the school you actually want.

Don't write an essay. Answer the question. One decision. What you did. Why faith was in the room.

Questions to get you started
  • Name one decision this year where faith actually changed what you did — not a decision you'd have made anyway.
  • What's something you believe that costs you something?
  • When you prepare to preach, what are you trying to do to the people listening?
  • Where have you doubted, and what did you do with it? (Doubt is not disqualifying at Notre Dame. It's interesting.)
  • What would someone who's watched you closely say your faith looks like in practice — not in belief?

Failure mode: describing faith in general. They know what faith is. They want to know what yours does.

Notre Dame — the rest

Required essay · 150 words

"Everyone has different priorities when considering their higher education options and building their college or university list. Tell us about your 'non-negotiable' factor(s) when searching for your future college home."
What they're really asking & how to get in

Really asking: do you know yourself well enough to have actual criteria — and do those criteria happen to describe us?

Failure mode: listing what's great about Notre Dame. They know. The essay is about you, and the fit should be obvious without you saying it.

Angles that fit your file:

  • A place where faith is discussed, not assumed — you've been doing that work already
  • Somewhere you can study science seriously and still sing seriously, without picking
  • A community small enough that showing up changes something
  • Somewhere service is structural, not an extracurricular

150 words is short. One non-negotiable, done well, beats three listed.

Second short answer · 50–100 words · pick one

2. "What is distinctive about your personal experiences and development (eg, family support, culture, disability, personal background, community)? Why are these experiences important to you and how will you enrich the Notre Dame community?"
3. "Notre Dame's undergraduate experience is characterized by a collective sense of care for every person. How do you foster service to others in your community?"
4. "What would you fight for?"
Which one to pick

#4 is the sleeper. Almost nobody answers it well, and it's the one where a performer with a preaching background can actually land a punch in 80 words. It rewards conviction and specificity.

#3 is safe and you have real material (NHS, preaching team). Risk: everyone answers this one, so it's crowded.

#2 only if there's something genuinely specific about your background you want them to know. Don't force it.

Whichever you pick, it should do something the faith answer doesn't. Two answers about service is a waste of one of them.

Common App personal statement · 650 words

This one goes to all nine schools, so it's the most valuable thing you'll write. It's also the hardest, which is why it is not first on your list. Do the Notre Dame short answers first and come back with momentum.

The deadline that matters: good enough to build on by Sept 30. Not finished. Not perfect. Good enough that supplements can start.
You already have four rough drafts. Start there.

Before writing anything new, go through what you've got and mark each one:

  • Alive — there's a real thing in here even if the writing is bad
  • Dead — the topic itself isn't going anywhere
  • Wrong shape — good material, wrong story around it

One "alive" draft saves you two weeks. Be honest, not loyal.

If you're starting fresh — questions, not prompts
  • What's something you were wrong about, and what changed your mind?
  • When have you been the only one who noticed something?
  • What do you do when nobody assigns it?
  • Describe a moment on stage — or preaching — where something real happened that wasn't in the script.
  • What's a thing you're good at that you didn't choose?
  • Where do the pieces of you not fit together? (Science and jazz. Softball and preaching. That tension is usually the essay.)

What makes these work: a small, specific moment you can actually see, and then honest thinking about it. Not an achievement list. Not a lesson learned.

Everything else

Most other prompts aren't public. They're only visible inside the Common App once you add the school. That's job #2 on your Now list — twenty minutes, and it unlocks the rest of this page.
Fordham · optional · 300 words · pick 1 of 3 VERIFY IN COMMON APP
A. "Our motto is 'New York is my campus, Fordham is my school.' … What has prepared you to embrace the unique opportunity of living and learning in New York City?"
B. "At Fordham, we have a tradition of having our graduating seniors send a message to their younger selves. What will the future 'you' tell the 'you' of today about what you accomplished as a Fordham student?"
C. "At Fordham, we expect students to care for and engage with their communities … Please share an experience you had that caused you to develop a new perspective, change your point of view, and/or empower you to take an action…"

It says optional. Do it anyway. Optional means optional for them, not for you. C is closest to material you already have.

UW–Madison · "Why Madison" VERIFY IN COMMON APP

Reuse warning: this cannot be your Notre Dame non-negotiables essay with the name swapped. Readers catch it instantly.

Real material you have: you spent a summer there for vocal performance. You know the campus. Almost no applicant can say that. Use it — specifically.

Madison's jazz ensembles are explicitly open to any student regardless of degree path. That's a real, checkable reason to want to be there, and it's about you.

Minnesota · two 150-word prompts? CONFLICT — CHECK COMMON APP

Minnesota's own checklist says essays are not required for admission. Other sources list two 150-word prompts. Check the Common App and find out which is true — this is a real fork in how much work Minnesota is.

The two reported prompts, unverified: what you'd like to study and why; and what you'd want them to know beyond academics.

The one question that decides everything

Does Notre Dame's free tuition actually apply?

Notre Dame covers full tuition for families under $150,000 — but they require the Noncustodial Parent CSS Profile. That means both parents' incomes get counted together, not just the one you live with.

Nobody can answer this until both profiles are filed. It's the difference between Notre Dame being the cheapest school on this list and one of the most expensive. Parents' job, not yours.

Music money — read this once

Nearly every music scholarship requires declaring the music major. Holy Cross's Brooks scholarship is full tuition, majors only. Madison's waiver, majors only. NYU Steinhardt, majors only.

What's still open to you without majoring: singing in the ensembles — which is open basically everywhere, including Madison's jazz groups and Holy Cross's jazz combo. And UW–Eau Claire, where jazz is fully open to every student.

What this means: you don't need to chase auditions at eight schools this fall. That whole workstream is off your plate.

Emails you can steal

Asking a music department a question

Subject: Prescreen timing — prospective first-year, jazz voice

Hi — I'm a senior applying for fall 2027 and I'm interested in jazz voice. Two questions: when is the prescreen deadline for next fall's auditions, and are students who aren't music majors eligible to audition for scholarships or ensembles? Thank you — [name], [high school], [city]

Short. Two specific questions. They answer short emails.

Requesting an interview

Subject: Interview request — first-year applicant

Hi — I'm applying as a first-year student for fall 2027 and I'd like to request an interview. I'm available [two or three real windows]. Thank you — [name], [high school], [city]

Send this in October. Slots fill.

What NOT to send

Don't email to say you submitted. Don't email to say you're still interested with nothing else in the message. Don't email weekly. Contact them when you have a real question or real news — that's it. Anything else reads as anxious, and admissions offices notice.

The December decision — Notre Dame answers

Yes → Holy Cross ED II is off. Apply Regular there if you want two aid offers to compare.

No → Holy Cross ED II is your strongest move, if the cost works.

Deferred → the hard one. You're still alive at Notre Dame, but ED II is binding — get in at Holy Cross and you withdraw from Notre Dame before they ever give you a real answer. That's a values question, not a strategy question, and it's yours.

The one rule: don't commit to anything binding until you know what it costs.
